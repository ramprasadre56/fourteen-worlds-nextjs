by Charu Bali


We often hear that the Lord resides within our heart as Paramatma, yet this truth is rarely experienced in the course of our daily lives. In the teachings of the Bhagavad-gita, Lord Krishna describes Paramatma—the Supersoul—as the witness, the permitter, and the guide seated within the heart. Upadrashtanumanta ca bharta bhokta mahesvarah (Bhagavad-gita 13.23)


We may have heard this many times. Yet, the real question remains—how does this truth become realized in our daily life?


Realization does not begin with something extraordinary; it begins with awareness. In the midst of a busy day, when a thought arises, we may pause and reflect: Who is witnessing this thought?


When confusion gives way to clarity, when there is a subtle inner check before acting wrongly, when something within gently guides us toward the right course—this quiet presence is Paramatma, the Supersoul guiding from within.


A simple practice is to cultivate inner remembrance: “Krishna is with me… within my heart… guiding me.” Not artificially, not forcefully, but with sincerity.


The presence of the Lord is not forced—it is revealed when the heart becomes receptive through bhakti. Krishna assures: Tesham satata-yuktanam… dadami buddhi-yogam tam (Bhagavad-gita 10.10). To those engaged in devotional service, He gives the intelligence by which they can come to Him. Gradually, one begins to perceive His presence through sincere chanting of the holy names, through humble service, and in moments of quiet remembrance. Often, this presence is experienced not dramatically, but as causeless peace, inner strength, and gentle guidance.


The Supreme Lord reciprocates according to one’s consciousness. Ye yatha mam prapadyante tams tathaiva bhajamy aham: “As all surrender unto Me, I reward them accordingly.” (Bhagavad-gita 4.11)


For a devotee, everything becomes the Lord’s mercy—even difficulties purify the heart and deepen surrender. For those who turn away, He acts through material nature as correction. And for the paramahamsas, the most elevated devotees, He is complete fulfilment—nothing beyond Him remains to be desired, as reflected in the Srimad-Bhagavatam (2.4.13).


Thus, the same Krishna is experienced differently—like sunlight: gentle, harsh, or illuminating, according to one’s disposition.


When distress comes, the teachings of bhakti become deeply personal. Krishna reminds us: Matra-sparsas tu kaunteya sitoshgna-sukha-duhkha-dah: “O son of Kunti, the nonpermanent appearance of happiness and distress, and their disappearance in due course, are like the appearance and disappearance of winter and summer seasons.” (Bhagavad-gita 2.14) A devotee gradually learns not merely to endure, but to offer: “Krishna, this is also Your mercy.”


In this shift, the question changes from “Why is this happening to me?” to “What is Krishna teaching me through this?” In this way, suffering becomes purification and deepens one’s dependence on Him.


In devotional service, one may begin with effort—and this is natural. Krishna describes a progression: Abhyase ’py asamartho ’si: “If you cannot practice the regulations of bhakti-yoga . . .” (Bhagavad-gita 12.9–12)


Through practice, effort becomes steadiness, and steadiness matures into devotion and ultimately love. What begins as duty gradually becomes a heartfelt desire to please Krishna and guru.


Thus, effort is not separate from love—it is the beginning of love. A simple prayer we may hold is: “Even if this feels like effort today, may it become loving devotional service tomorrow.”


Paramatma is never far away. He is the one who hears our prayers even before they are fully formed. Indeed, the very longing to realize Him is His presence within the heart. When we begin to notice Him in small, sincere moments, life transforms—from routine to relationship, from isolation to divine companionship, from effort to loving devotion.


The unseen companion was never absent. He was always present within the heart — waiting to be realized.
