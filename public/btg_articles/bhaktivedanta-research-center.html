Undertakes Preservation Service at


Srila Prabhupada’s Room in Sridhama Mayapur


Restoring the Library That Quietly Shaped a Global Spiritual Revolution


By Kulavati Krishnapriya Devi Dasi and Dr. Sumanta Rudra


Within the Lotus Building of Sridhama Mayapur, preserved with extraordinary care across several decades, stands the personal room of His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada. The kerosene lamp by which he worked through the night remains in its place. His japa-mala, dictaphone, footwear, thermos, shaving accessories, and tongue cleaner are all carefully preserved exactly as they were during his physical presence. One can also still see the cup from which Srila Prabhupada drank water, his white walking-stick umbrella with its rounded wooden handle, and the personal altar photograph of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Öhakura that he worshipped daily.


The room further preserves numerous kitchen and household items connected to his daily life and service, including his chapati pan, spice blender, metal cooking pots, stone mortar for grinding spices, kitchen ladles, and wooden stand used for preparing chapatis for both Srila Prabhupada and the Deities. Nearby, an almirah still contains his bedsheets, mosquito net, and other personal belongings, while a safe carefully guards important documents and correspondence connected to his mission.


Among these preserved belongings is a collection of rare books—not authored by Srila Prabhupada, but personally consulted by him which include rare Sanskrit treatises, authoritative Bengali commentaries of the previous acaryas, and scholarly works in Hindi and English, which he studied regularly to ensure that every word of his purports remained firmly aligned with the unbroken disciplic succession.


Comprising approximately thirty volumes, this collection may be understood, in the truest sense, as the “books behind the books”—the foundational sources underpinning one of the most remarkable literary and spiritual contributions of the modern era.


Today, through dedicated preservation efforts, these invaluable texts have been carefully conserved by the Bhaktivedanta Research Center for the benefit of future generations. In addition, a digital library has now been created, enabling devotees, scholars, and seekers worldwide to access these foundational works with ease.


In this way, what was once accessible only within the intimate space of Srila Prabhupada’s personal study is now being respectfully extended to a global audience, ensuring that the depth and authenticity of the Gauòiya Vaishnava tradition continue to illuminate hearts and minds for generations to come.


A DEVOTEE’S STEADFAST VIGILANCE


Vasanta Ranjani Devi Dasi has served in Srila Prabhupada’s room for seventeen years washing his garments, dressing the Deity of Gauranga, maintaining the place diligently.


Over years of observation, she witnessed the gradual deterioration of these volumes—spines weakening, pages growing fragile—their invaluable intellectual and spiritual legacy quietly fading with time.


“For years I was searching,” she has since shared, her words carrying the quiet urgency of a devotee who has long held a concern close to the heart. She did not abandon that search. And in due course, as is so often the arrangement of the Lord, the right connection was made.


AN AUSPICIOUS ENTRUSTMENT


On December 11, 2025, His Grace Jananivasa Prabhu formally entrusted the Bhaktivedanta Research Center (BRC) with the seva of preserving and digitizing the collection. The connection was initiated through Milan Madhava Dasa, the assistant of Jananivasa Prabhu, who reached out to BRC with the assistance of Aradhya Bhagavan Dasa. The Bhaktivedanta Research Center accepted the responsibility without hesitation.


What followed was several months of disciplined work. Each of the first twelve volumes selected for the initial phase was professionally rebound using archival-quality materials specifically designed for long-term preservation. Spines were carefully embossed with appropriate identification, ensuring that each volume could be recognized immediately while its dignity remained fully intact. Every page was subjected to high-resolution scanning, and a dedicated digital archive was constructed to house the results.


A CEREMONY BEFITTING SACRED TEXTS


On February 9, 2026, on the occasion of Prabhupada Connect Day, the twelve restored volumes were formally presented to the ISKCON community.


The day commenced with a Srimad-Bhagavatam class, followed by a discourse by His Grace Gauranga Dasa, Director of the BRC, on the significance of Srila Prabhupada’s room and the preservation initiative it inspired. The restored volumes were then placed on a palanquin and carried in procession to the Lotus Building, where they were received with a formal puja and pushpanjali ceremony, as devotees offered flowers and prayers in welcome.


As a further mark of honour, new metallic plaques were installed within Srila Prabhupada’s room, replacing the earlier acrylic ones and providing a more permanent and dignified identification of the preserved items.


When Vasanta Ranjani Devi held one of the beautifully restored volumes in her hands, she described it in words that carried both simplicity and depth: “Ancient knowledge in a new cover.” She added, with characteristic humility, that Srila Prabhupada “appreciated everything being kept very clean, very nicely done for Krishna,” and that he deserves nothing less than the very best that devoted service can offer.


THE PRINCIPLE THAT ANIMATED THE PRACTICE


To understand the significance of this collection, one must appreciate the scholarly and devotional discipline that governed the method of composition of Srila Prabhupada.


In his purport to Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.2.12, he clearly states, “Unless, therefore, devotional service is established on the principles of sruti, smriti, purana or pancaratra authorities, the make-show of devotional service should at once be rejected.” Similarly, in Chaitanya-caritamrita (Adi-lila 7.48, purport), he emphasizes, “In the parampara system, the instructions taken from the bona fide spiritual master must also be based on revealed Vedic scriptures. One who is in the line of disciplic succession cannot manufacture his own way of behavior.”


This was not merely a principle he taught but it was one he embodied with unwavering determination. Each evening, before committing his thoughts to paper or dictaphone, he would consult these very volumes, tracing the thread of meaning through the parampara. Jananivasa Prabhu has recounted how these books accompanied him, especially during his stays in Vrindavana and other holy places; they were, in the truest sense, his companions in service.


A SACRED SPACE MADE UNIVERSALLY ACCESSIBLE


Among the most meaningful dimensions of this initiative is the breadth of access it now makes possible.


The BRC has launched a dedicated digital archive — available at prabhupadaslibrary.org — through which scholars, researchers, and devotees across the world may explore the collection in its entirety. The platform has been designed so that visitors may navigate the volumes page by page, study the precise editions Srila Prabhupada consulted, examine the commentarial traditions he drew upon, and observe any notations or markings made in his own hand.


For those who are able to come to Mayapur in person, the originals — rebound, embossed, and safely housed — remain in their place within Srila Prabhupada’s room, available for darsana alongside the lamp, the mala, and the other personal effects that have defined this sacred space for nearly half a century.


THE WORK THAT REMAINS


The complete collection comprises approximately thirty volumes. The first phase of restoration — twelve volumes, preserved and digitized to the highest archival standards — has been formally completed. The BRC has committed to continuing this work in successive phases until every remaining volume has received equivalent care.


“Our goal has been to preserve them with utmost care while making them accessible to devotees and scholars worldwide,” stated Gauranga Dasa. The institutional commitment behind those words is borne out by the quality of the work already accomplished — work that reflects both scholarly precision and a clear understanding of the spiritual gravity of what these volumes represent.


The ongoing stewardship of Srila Prabhupada’s room by Jananivasa Prabhu, supported faithfully by Nirmal Nitai Dasa, Vasanta Ranjani Devi, and the dedicated team of sevaites, has created the conditions in which this preservation work is possible. That stwardship, combined with the institutional support of ISKCON Mayapur and the archival expertise of the BRC, has produced a result that honours the full significance — material, intellectual, and transcendental — of what these books represent.
