Do you see the rains as a seasonal routine?


Why does the moon release its cooling rays?


Or the monsoon cloud its soothing rains?


Or a munificent man his wealth?


Each delivers relief.


But today let’s focus on the rains. With the arrival of the monsoon, the earth seems to awaken from a long austerity. Fields that lay dry and cracked begin to soften. Rivers swell with renewed vigor. The fragrance of wet soil rises into the air like incense offered by the earth herself. For the awakened mind, this transformation is more than a seasonal change; it is a reminder of the ever-present grace of the Supreme Lord Sri Sri Krishna.


In the Bhagavad Gita, Sri Krishna declares that all natural processes ultimately rest upon Him. He explains that from food beings are sustained, from rain food is produced, and from sacrifice comes rain. This cycle of nature is therefore not mechanical but sacred. The monsoon becomes a visible expression of the cosmic order established and maintained personally by Him.


After months of heat, the first clouds gather on the horizon. To the materialist, they may appear as mere atmospheric phenomena. To the devotee, they reveal a deeper lesson. Just as the clouds collect water from the oceans and distribute it freely over the land, the saintly and compassionate souls receive spiritual wisdom from the Supreme and shower it upon all living beings without discrimination. The cloud does not choose one field over another; it nourishes all. In the same way, genuine spiritual teachers distribute divine knowledge for the welfare of every soul.


The Srimad-Bhagavatam often draws profound spiritual insights from the rainy season. The dark rain clouds are compared to the beautiful complexion of Sri Krishna. Lightning flashing across the sky evokes His dazzling ornaments. The peacocks dance upon seeing the clouds, just as devotees rejoice when they receive the association of the Lord and His devotees. Nature itself becomes a vast temple, filled with symbols that direct the heart toward remembrance of God.


The monsoon also teaches humility. During the dry season, dust rises easily and covers everything. When the rains arrive, the dust settles and the atmosphere becomes clear. Similarly, the conditioned soul is often covered by the dust of pride, selfish desire, and forgetfulness of the Lord. The showers of divine remembrance cleanse the heart and allow one to see reality more clearly.


The overflowing rivers offer another lesson. Water naturally flows toward the ocean, its ultimate destination. Likewise, every soul seeks fulfillment in serving the lotus feet of Sri Krishna. Temporary pursuits may provide brief satisfaction, but the heart remains restless until it reconnects with its eternal source.


There is also a lesson in patience. For months the earth waits beneath the blazing sun. The farmer prepares his field long before the rains arrive. Then, at the proper time, the clouds appear and abundance follows. Spiritual life unfolds in a similar way. One may practice devotion for years without immediately perceiving the fruit. Yet sincere effort is never lost. When the heart has been properly cultivated through prayer, study, service, and remembrance, divine grace descends like life-giving rain.


The beginning of the rainy season therefore invites reflection and gratitude. Every drop that falls from the sky proclaims Sri Krishna’s sustaining presence. Every green shoot that emerges from the soil bears witness to His mercy. As the clouds gather and the earth is renewed, may we remember the source of all nourishment, both material and spiritual. And may the rains inspire us to cultivate a heart fertile enough to receive the blessings that eternally flow from the Supreme.


—Syamananda Dasa
