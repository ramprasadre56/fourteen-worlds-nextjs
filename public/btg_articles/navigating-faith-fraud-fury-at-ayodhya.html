The inauguration of the Rama Lalla Mandir at Ayodhya Dhama was a watershed moment. Centuries old religious passions were ignited. Ram bhaktas throng in millions. Donations start pouring in. And then the inevitable happens: A few black sheep ruin the reputation, the devotional bhava (sentiments) of the government authorities as well as the masses, by stealing offerings meant for the Lord.

What went wrong?


Rupa Gosvami built Radha Govindaji’s grand temple in Sri Vrindavan dhama by engaging a king’s wealth — yet he himself admonished us: “Do not be overly enthusiastic about erecting costly temples.” The caution is clear — let devotion lead infrastructure, not the other way around.”

Five key takeaways from this current news item:

1. Bhakti is real. Black sheep are not new


By constructing the grand Rama Mandir at the spot where it had stood for hundreds of years, centuries of suppressed Ram-bhakti had finally found expression. Millions thronging, donating gold, diamonds, and cash is not blind superstition; rather it is a civilizational release of expression after 500 years.

And at the same time, however unpleasant it may sound, the theft of offerings has happened in every era (even in ancient temples mentioned in Puranas and medieval records). But human greed doesn’t invalidate divine aspiration.

2. Separate the Temple from the Temple-Management


The arca-vigraha or murti of Ram Lalla (Lord Rama as a small child) is the living centre of devotion. The trust, the officials, and security apparatus are worldly institutions run by fallible humans. Therefore anger against them is justified, but directing it only at the Mandir is spiritual shortsightedness. This is exactly how reformers in the past (including some bhakti saints) critiqued corruption without abandoning the Deity.

Some suggested action points: Demand CCTV, independent audits, public dashboards of donations and expenditures. Support constructive pressure, not blanket rejection.

3. Justice is dharma. Cancellation is not

Those stealing from Mandir are committing maha-papa (a great sin). Call for swift police action, fast-track courts, and implement exemplary punishment. Devotees need to channel outrage into petitions, RTI, and social media amplification for transparency.

Those who are pushing the “abolish temple offerings culture” are recycling colonial and Marxist critiques that weakened Hindu institutions for more than 200 years. They ignore how dana (temple charity), seva (volunteering), and offerings sustained temples, mathas, and dharma during invasions and neglect.

A thriving temple economy funds education, healthcare, and culture. Empty temples become ruins as history proves. Reform the management, don’t abolish the culture.

4. A growing class of young Indians have shown their merit in IT industry (and many others as well). And as professionals live by precision, accountability, and merit, we need to apply the same standards here: insist on corporate-style governance for large temples (audited accounts, conflict-of-interest rules, professional managers).

5. Channel rage into regeneration

This is not the end of the Ram Mandir movement — it is a call for the next phase: from emotional upsurge to institutional maturity.

History shows Hindu society has repeatedly self-corrected (Sankaracarya reforms, bhakti movement led by Tulsidas, Tukarama, Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu) modern temple entry and management acts. Let us all become part of that correction. Don’t let a few thieves turn your sraddha (devotional faith) into cynicism. Let it fuel vigilance. Rama-rajya is not delivered by one temple. It is built by millions of Rama-bhaktas who refuse to let darkness win — inside the temple or inside their hearts.

—Syamananda Dasa
